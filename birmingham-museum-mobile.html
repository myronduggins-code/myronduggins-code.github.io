UNIVERSAL ACCESS

Designing a museum mobile app everyone can navigate, in their own language

I designed a mobile app for Birmingham Museum & Art Gallery that helps visitors explore exhibitions, events, book tickets, and stay informed even if the visitors aren’t confident with technology or English isn’t their first language.

In this project I set out to design an app that works well for busy parents looking for a family-friendly afternoon out and also for new arrivals to the city. It is built to be simple to use, translatable, and easy to keep up with.

THE CHALLENGE

How might we make a museum mobile app approachable for visitors who find websites, language, or technology a barrier?

The app needed to address two sources of stress: not knowing how to use a mobile app very well and not being able to fully read it. Every decision followed one goal, could someone find and book something they wanted to see without friction getting in the way?

THE PROBLEM

The existing museum app wasn't user-friendly, had no translation feature for non-English speakers or readers, and gave visitors no way to get email reminders for upcoming or booked events.

THE GOAL

Make the mobile app streamlined and simple to use, add a translation option so it's usable in more than one language, and let visitors opt in to email reminders for the events they are interested in.

RESEARCH DISCOVERY

Research combined secondary user demographic data with AI-assisted synthesis to identify the primary friction points people face using the current mobile app.

PAIN POINTS UNCOVERED IN PRIMARY RESEARCH

01

THE MOBILE APP WASN’T USER-FRIENDLY

Focus on making the design as streamlined and simple as possible.

02

NO TRANSLATION

Incorporate a translation feature so people from different countries can understand and use the app.

03

NO EMAIL REMINDERS

Add email reminders for upcoming events, and for events people have already booked.

REFINING THE SYSTEM

Mock-ups, a high-fidelity prototype, and a second round of testing

A second usability round on the high-fidelity design highlighted more issues from icon recognition to text contrast and consistency across screens.

Before: Testing found the language and home icons weren't recognised by a user.

After: Both icons were replaced with clearer, more familiar equivalents.

After: Placeholder text was replaced with real content for clearer text and the paint stroke was sharpened for better contrast.

ACCESSIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS

1

Users can always get back to the home screen via the home icon in the nav bar and a dedicated home button on the confirmation page.

2

A language selection option lets visitors use the app in their own language if they find English difficult.

TAKEAWAYS NEXT STEPS

“The app was straight to the point and it wasn't confusing to use.”

Running two full rounds of usability testing showed how much a design changes once real participants use it, from icon recognition, text contrast, and animation consistency these only became apparent once people were actually navigating the flow themselves. Staying open to that kind of feedback, even late in the process, made the final design better.
